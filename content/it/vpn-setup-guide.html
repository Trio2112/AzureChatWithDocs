VPN Setup Guide

Overview

Contoso uses GlobalProtect to provide secure remote access to internal systems, including source control, internal wikis, and HR systems. This guide covers installing the client, connecting for the first time, understanding how traffic is routed while connected, and troubleshooting common connection issues. All remote employees are required to use the VPN when accessing internal resources, per the Remote Work Policy and the Acceptable Use and Security Policy.

Prerequisites

  • A Contoso-issued laptop with the standard software image installed.
  • An active corporate email account and password.
  • The Microsoft Authenticator app installed on your phone for multi-factor authentication.
  • A stable internet connection of at least 10 Mbps.

If you are a new hire and have not yet received your laptop or completed initial account setup, see the New Hire IT Equipment and Software Setup guide before proceeding with VPN configuration.

Installing the client

Download the GlobalProtect installer from the software portal at software.contoso.internal. Windows users should run GlobalProtect64.msi with default settings; macOS users should run GlobalProtect.pkg. When prompted for a gateway address during first launch, enter vpn.contoso.internal. Restart your laptop after installation completes to ensure the network extension loads correctly.

Connecting for the first time

Open GlobalProtect from the system tray (Windows) or menu bar (macOS) and sign in with your corporate email and password. You will be prompted for multi-factor authentication via the Authenticator app on your first connection of the day. Once connected, the icon in your system tray turns green and displays "Connected." Connection typically takes under 15 seconds on a stable network.

The first time you connect, GlobalProtect will also install a root certificate that allows the client to inspect traffic for security purposes. This is expected behavior and required for compliance with our security policy.

How traffic is routed

By default, Contoso's VPN configuration disables split-tunneling, meaning all network traffic from your device is routed through the corporate network while connected, not just traffic destined for internal systems. This allows security monitoring to apply consistently regardless of where you are working from, but it also means general web browsing may feel slightly slower while connected. This is expected and should not be worked around by disconnecting the VPN during work hours.

Troubleshooting

"Unable to connect" error

First confirm you have an active internet connection independent of the VPN (for example, by loading a public website). If your internet connection is fine but the VPN still won't connect, restart the GlobalProtect client from the system tray menu.

Client hangs on "Connecting..."

This is usually caused by a stale cached credential. Clear the client's cached credentials via Settings > Reset, then reconnect and re-authenticate from scratch. If the issue persists after a reset, restart your laptop.

MFA prompt never arrives

Confirm your phone has a working internet or cellular connection, since Authenticator push notifications require connectivity. If no prompt arrives within two minutes, open the Authenticator app manually and check for a pending request under the Contoso entry.

Connected, but internal sites won't load

Confirm the tray icon shows "Connected" and not "Connecting" or "Disconnected." If connected but sites still fail to load, try disconnecting and reconnecting once. Persistent failures across multiple internal sites usually indicate a gateway issue on our end rather than a problem with your device — check the IT status page before opening a ticket.

Getting further help

For persistent failures not resolved by the steps above, open a ticket with the IT help desk through the process described in the Help Desk Ticketing and Support Guide, and include the error code shown in the client logs (Settings > Troubleshooting > Collect Logs). Standard VPN connectivity issues are handled within our normal Severity 3 SLA.
